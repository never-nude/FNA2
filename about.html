About Fisher Hill.

Fisher Hill is a historic neighborhood within walking distance of the central business district of White Plains, New York. Residential development began around 1900, and many of the large vintage homes from that era still define the neighborhood's character today. Fisher Hill is notably diverse in ethnicity, age, and income.

The neighborhood is bounded by the Bronx River Parkway to the west and Post Road to the east, with White Plains Hospital, the public library, the Metro-North station, and City Center all just beyond its edges. The Fisher Hill Association represents residents within the White Plains Council of Neighborhood Associations (WPCNA).

Fisher Hill & the Battle of White Plains.

On October 28, 1776, the Battle of White Plains was fought just west of here. The day's decisive action came on Chatterton's Hill — the height that today's Battle Hill neighborhood is named for — across the Bronx River. General Howe massed some twenty cannon and sent roughly four thousand British and Hessian troops, who crossed the rain-swollen Bronx River and stormed the slope, breaking the right of Washington's line and forcing the Continental Army to withdraw northward.

Fisher Hill sits on the east bank of the Bronx River — the side from which the British advanced and positioned their guns — so the neighborhood's ground lay within the eastern approach to that assault. Local tradition holds that British artillery looked out from these heights toward Battle Hill; period records name Chatterton's Hill as the objective, but the geography fits the story.
